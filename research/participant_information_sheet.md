# Participant Information Sheet — DRAFT (requires ethics approval; adapt to your university's template)

**Study title:** How do developers decide whether to trust AI-generated code?
**Researcher:** [Student name], [Programme], [University] · **Supervisor:** [Name, email]
**Ethics reference:** [to be added after approval]

**What is the study about?** We are studying how developers review code written by AI assistants and how different
kinds of information affect their decisions.

**What will I do?** In one session (about 60 minutes) you will review 16 short pieces of AI-generated Python or JavaScript
code in VS Code. For each, you decide whether to accept, modify or reject it and say how confident you are. Sometimes you
will see extra information from a tool. You will also answer short questionnaires about workload, trust and usability.

**Do I have to take part?** No. Participation is voluntary. You can stop at any time without giving a reason, and you can
withdraw your data until [date] by giving your participant code. Taking part or not has no effect on your grades or employment.

**What data are collected?** Your decisions, confidence ratings, response times, interactions with the tool (for example,
which sections you expanded), questionnaire answers, your self-reported experience level and years of experience.
We do **not** collect your name in the study data, your own code, or your screen or camera.

**How are data protected?** Data are stored under a random participant code on [secure university storage], kept for
[retention period], and reported only in aggregate or as anonymous quotations. Please do not include personal information in free-text answers.

**Risks and benefits.** No foreseeable risks beyond mild fatigue. You may learn about common defects in AI-generated code.
A summary of the findings is available on request.
